For flight simulation only GA-EFB is made for Microsoft Flight Simulator. Its weather, NOTAMs, charts, airspace, routes, fuel, weight and balance and performance figures are for use in the sim, and aren't approved or kept up to date for real aviation. Never use it to plan or fly a real aircraft.

1. What you need

  • A Windows 10 or 11 PC (64-bit) with Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024, connected to the internet.
  • An iPad (or iPhone, Android tablet or another computer) on the same Wi-Fi as the PC. You can also use GA-EFB in a window on the PC itself.
  • An email address, for your free GA-EFB account.

GA-EFB has two parts. A small program runs on the PC you fly on and talks to the sim. Your iPad opens it in Safari over your home Wi-Fi, so there's nothing to install from the App Store.

2. Install it on your PC

  1. Download the GA-EFB Launcher from ga-efb.co.uk and open it. If Windows SmartScreen warns you about it, choose More info, then Run anyway.
  2. Create your GA-EFB account in the Launcher: your email and a password. We email you a link (check junk mail too). Once you click it, the Launcher signs you in.
  3. Press Install. It needs no administrator password and nothing else, and takes a minute or two. GA-EFB sets itself to start with MSFS 2024, so from now on it's ready whenever you fly.
  4. Allow it through the Windows firewall. The first time GA-EFB starts, Windows may ask whether to allow Node.js on your network. Tick Private networks and press Allow. Without this, your iPad can't reach it.

The Launcher is your control panel on the PC. It shows whether GA-EFB is running and the address for your iPad, and it can open the EFB on the PC, check for updates, start GA-EFB if it isn't running, or buy GA-EFB. While GA-EFB runs, its icon sits in the system tray by the clock.

3. Open it on your iPad

  1. Find the address. The Launcher shows it next to Server running, for example iPad: http://192.168.1.20:4780. GA-EFB's Settings → About shows it too.
  2. Open it in Safari on your iPad: type the address exactly as shown, starting http://.
  3. Sign in with your GA-EFB account, the same email and password as in the Launcher.
  4. Add it to your home screen. In Safari, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen. From then on it opens full screen from its own icon, like an app.

GA-EFB opens on a lock screen, like an iPad's: swipe up (or tap) to unlock. The first time, it reminds you that it's for flight simulation only.

Tip: the address belongs to your PC on your home network. If your router ever gives the PC a new address, open the new one from the Launcher and add it to your home screen again. Setting a fixed (reserved) address for the PC in your router stops this happening.

4. Things to set up first

Open Settings on the EFB. These are all optional, but worth a minute:

  • Your profile (tap your name at the top): your name, your home airport, and your SimBrief username and VATSIM CID if you use them.
  • Flying Mode: VFR for light aircraft, with the plog, pre-flight, kneeboard and radio calls; or Airliner for SimBrief, VATSIM, GSX and landing reports. You can switch any time, and each mode keeps its own home screen.
  • Units: UK (kg, litres, hPa) is the default. Altitude, speed and distance are always feet, knots and nautical miles.
  • Weather & Limits: your own minimums for cloud base, visibility and crosswind, used by the weather go/no-go. A free OpenWeatherMap key adds the cloud layer to the maps (rain radar needs no key).
  • Maps & Data: a free openAIP key adds runways, declared distances, reporting points and airspace outside the UK.
  • Lock Screen & Passcode, Notifications, Display & Brightness (dark, light, or Night Red for night flying), Wallpaper and Home Screen & Dock: make it yours.
  • Simulator shows whether GA-EFB can see MSFS (it connects by itself when the sim is running), and turns the warnings about airspace ahead on or off.

5. Plan and fly a VFR flight

The Pre-flight app takes you through everything before a flight, ticking each part off as you go. You can also open each app on its own.

  1. Route — VFR Plog. Enter your departure and destination and let GA-EFB suggest a route by reporting points, round controlled airspace, danger areas and ATZs. Or build your own, or import one from Little Navmap or SkyDemon. The route check shows the airspace and terrain along it, with a suggested altitude. The plog then works out headings, times and fuel with forecast winds.
  2. Weather. METARs and TAFs for your departure, destination and alternates, decoded and checked against your limits: GO, CAUTION or NO-GO.
  3. NOTAMs along your route, from the UK NOTAM feed.
  4. Weight & Balance. Pick the aircraft, or let GA-EFB read it from the sim. Enter people, baggage and fuel (the plog's fuel can come straight across) and check the weight and centre of gravity against the envelope. The take-off and landing tabs work out the distance you need for the runway, surface and weather.
  5. Daylight at your destination, so you're down before it's dark.
  6. Checklists for the aircraft you're flying, from the pre-flight inspection to shutdown, with the emergencies one tap away.

In the air, GA-EFB follows you from the sim:

  • The Kneeboard opens by itself on take-off: your next waypoint, heading, times, fuel and endurance, FREDA and fuel checks when they're due, who to talk to, and a scratchpad.
  • The VFR Plog follows you leg by leg, with your actual times and fuel against the plan.
  • The Map shows your aircraft, your route, airports and their layouts, with rain and cloud. GA-EFB warns you about controlled airspace and danger areas on your track a few minutes ahead (you can turn this off in Settings → Simulator).
  • Radio Calls gives you the UK calls for each part of the flight, filled in with your callsign, position, altitude and QNH.
  • Diversion lists the nearest airfields with bearing and distance, if you need one.

After landing, the Flight Tracker writes the flight into your Logbook by itself, with times, landings and night, and a report on each touchdown: rate, G, centreline and distance down the runway.

6. Circuit training

For circuits instead of a cross-country, open the VFR Plog and choose Circuits at the top. Your cross-country route is kept, and comes back when you switch back.

  1. Pick the airfield: type its code or name (any airfield), or tap your home airfield.
  2. The runway is the one most into wind, using the sim's own wind when you're there with it running, else the METAR. Each runway shows its headwind and crosswind, so you can pick another. A runway missing (small airfields' grass strips sometimes are)? Tap A runway missing? and add it: its number, length and surface.
  3. Check the circuit direction and height. The default is left-hand at 1,000 ft above the airfield. Where openAIP records the direction (with your openAIP key) it's used instead. The real circuit for each runway is in the airfield's AIP entry or on its website: if it's different, change it once, and GA-EFB remembers it for that runway on all your devices. It also works out the other end of the runway, since the circuit usually stays on the same side (set 28 left-hand and 10 becomes right-hand), and a grass strip beside it.
  4. Plan the circuits: how many, and what each one is — normal, flapless, glide approach or go-around. The last one ends with a full-stop landing.

GA-EFB draws the circuit with the deadside, the overhead join and the ATZ, and works out each leg's heading for the wind, height, speed and time, plus the fuel for the session. Pre-flight changes to suit: the weather is checked against your limits for the crosswind on your runway and the cloud base against circuit height, and the airspace and NOTAMs are checked round the circuit.

In the air, the Kneeboard shows which leg you're on, the circuit number, the heading, height and speed to fly, what to do next, and the radio call. Radio Calls has a Circuits tab too. After each circuit you get a short report: the height of the crosswind turn, your height and spacing on downwind, the height of the final turn, your speed on final, and the touchdown (rate, where on the runway, and the centreline). Touch-and-goes, go-arounds and the full stop are counted by themselves, and the session goes in your logbook.

8. Getting around the EFB

GA-EFB works like an iPad:

  • Home screen: app icons, the dock along the bottom, and widgets for today's flight, the weather and pre-flight. Press and hold to move icons, make folders or change widgets.
  • Search: swipe down on the home screen to find apps, settings, airports, your saved plans and documents.
  • Control Centre: pull down the status bar at the top for the Flying Mode, brightness, Night Red, wallpaper and lock, whether the sim and GA-EFB are connected, and your notifications. Swipe it up to put it away.
  • Notifications remind you of your departure time and sunset, warn you about airspace ahead and bring you each landing report. Red badges on app icons show what needs a look.

9. Your account and your devices

  • The same everywhere. Your logbook, notes, plogs, checklists and settings go with your GA-EFB account, so sign in on your iPad, iPhone and PC and they all show the same things.
  • One account per PC. A PC's copy of GA-EFB belongs to the account signed in to its Launcher. Other accounts can't use it, and are told whose it is. If you sign the Launcher in to a different account, your own things are put safely aside on the PC and come back when you sign in again.
  • Backups. Settings → Back Up & Restore saves everything (including your photos, wallpaper and charts) to one file, and puts it back.
  • Forgotten your password? Use Forgot password in the Launcher: we email you a code to set a new one. Settings → your name lets you change it, or sign out of all your devices.

10. Updates

GA-EFB updates itself, like an iPad. When a new version is out, it installs it on the PC while you're not using it (never in the middle of a flight). Then refresh the EFB on your iPad to see what's new. You can also press Update now in the Launcher, and turn automatic updates off in Settings → About.

11. Your trial and buying GA-EFB

  • Your 7-day free trial starts the first time you use the EFB. Settings → Licence shows when it ends.
  • Buy GA-EFB (£20, once) from the Launcher, Settings → Licence, or the website. It unlocks by itself within a few seconds, on all your devices. If you buy on the website, use your GA-EFB account's email address at the checkout.
  • A licence key (a gift, say)? Settings → Licence → I have a licence key.
  • Everything from your trial is kept. Changed your mind? You can have a refund within 14 days: see the refund policy.

12. Problems and how to fix them

My iPad can't open the address
  • Check the iPad and PC are on the same Wi-Fi (not a guest network or mobile data).
  • Check the Launcher says Server running. If not, press Start server.
  • Type the address exactly as the Launcher shows it, starting http:// (not https).
  • The Windows firewall may be blocking it. Open Windows Security → Firewall & network protection → Allow an app through firewall, find Node.js, and tick Private. Also check Windows treats your home Wi-Fi as a Private network (Settings → Network & internet → your Wi-Fi).
  • Your PC's address may have changed: use the one the Launcher shows now.
It says the simulator is offline

GA-EFB connects to MSFS 2024 by itself once the sim is running. Give it a few seconds, and check you're in a flight rather than the main menu. If it still says Offline, right-click GA-EFB's icon by the clock, choose Quit GA-EFB, then press Start server in the Launcher.

GA-EFB didn't start with the sim

Open the GA-EFB Launcher and press Start server. If you installed MSFS 2024 after GA-EFB, run the sim once and close it, then start GA-EFB from the Launcher: it sets itself to start with the sim again.

"This GA-EFB belongs to another account"

Each PC's GA-EFB belongs to the account signed in to its Launcher. Sign in on your iPad with that same account, or sign the Launcher in to yours.

I bought it but it hasn't unlocked

It usually takes a few seconds. Open the EFB again, or go to Settings → Licence. If you bought it on the website, check you used the same email address as your GA-EFB account. If you used a different one, email us with both addresses and we'll sort it out.

No clouds on the map

The cloud layer needs a free OpenWeatherMap key (Settings → Weather & Limits). New keys can take a couple of hours to start working. Rain radar works without one.

The EFB on my iPad looks out of date

After an update, close GA-EFB on the iPad and open it again (or pull down to refresh in Safari).

The Launcher shows "Log file" and "EFB folder" — what are they for?

If something goes wrong and you email us, the log file helps us see what happened. The EFB folder is where GA-EFB is installed. You won't need either day to day.
