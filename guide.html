For flight simulation only GA-EFB is made for Microsoft Flight Simulator. Its weather, NOTAMs, charts, airspace, routes, fuel, weight and balance and performance figures are for use in the sim, and aren't approved or kept up to date for real aviation. Never use it to plan or fly a real aircraft.

1. What you need

  • An iPad or iPhone, and an email address for your free GA-EFB account.
  • For the live data from the sim: a Windows 10 or 11 PC (64-bit) with Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024 and the free GA-EFB Connector, on the same Wi-Fi as your iPad or iPhone.

GA-EFB is an app. It plans, briefs and keeps your logbook on its own, anywhere. The free GA-EFB Connector, on the PC you fly on, adds what comes from the sim: your aircraft on the map, the plog and kneeboard following you, weight and fuel from the aircraft, the flight in the Logbook as it happens and landing reports, and GSX and add-on controls.

2. Get the app

  1. Download GA-EFB from the App Store on your iPad or iPhone.
  2. Create your GA-EFB account in the app: press Continue with Apple, or use your email and a password (we email you a code to confirm it; check junk mail too).
  3. Open it. GA-EFB opens straight in (if you've set a GA-EFB passcode in Settings → Passcode, it asks for that first). The first time, it reminds you that it's for flight simulation only.

Your 7-day free trial starts the first time you use it. Sign in with the same account on each of your iPads and iPhones.

3. Connect it to the sim

  1. Download the GA-EFB Connector on the PC you fly on and open GA-EFB-Connector-Setup.exe. If Windows SmartScreen warns you about it, choose More info, then Run anyway. It needs no administrator password, and starts by itself with Windows from then on.
  2. Allow it through the Windows firewall. The first time, Windows may ask whether to allow Node.js on your network. Tick Private networks and press Allow. Without this, your iPad can't reach it.
  3. Pair your iPad or iPhone: open GA-EFB Connector from the Start menu and press Pair an iPad or iPhone. In the app, go to Settings → Simulator → Scan pairing QR and scan the code within 90 seconds.
  4. It keeps running: closing the Connector's window leaves it in the notification area (by the clock), so the app gets the live data whenever MSFS runs. Right-click its icon to open it or quit.
  5. Updates: the Connector updates itself, never while MSFS is connected, and keeps your paired devices. If you'd rather choose when, turn off Install updates automatically in its window: it then tells you when a new version is out, and you press Update.

That's it: whenever the Connector and MSFS are running and your device is on the same Wi-Fi, the app shows the live data. Everything between them is encrypted with a key only your device and your PC have. Flights the Connector records go into the app's logbook the next time it's connected.

4. Things to set up first

Open Settings on the EFB. These are all optional, but worth a minute:

  • Your profile (tap your name at the top): your name, your home airport, and your SimBrief username if you use it.
  • Units: UK (kg, litres, hPa) is the default. Altitude, speed and distance are always feet, knots and nautical miles.
  • Weather & Limits: your own minimums for cloud base, visibility and crosswind, used by the weather go/no-go. A free OpenWeatherMap key gives a backup cloud layer for the maps if the satellite clouds aren't available.
  • Maps & Data: the map styles and data. openAIP's runways, declared distances, reporting points and airspace outside the UK are included, with no key needed.
  • Passcode (off unless you want one), Alerts, Appearance (dark or light, brightness, Night Red for night flying, and the home screen's background) and Home Layout: make it yours.
  • Simulator pairs the app with the GA-EFB Connector, shows whether it can see MSFS (it connects by itself when the sim is running), and turns the warnings about airspace ahead on or off.

5. Plan and fly a VFR flight

The Pre-flight app takes you through everything before a flight, ticking each part off as you go. You can also open each app on its own.

  1. Route — the Map's Plan mode. Open the Map and tap Plan at the top. Type where you're flying From and To (or tap the airfields on the map: Set as departure, Set as destination), and GA-EFB fills in a route by reporting points round controlled airspace, danger areas and ATZs at your altitude (Undo beside "Suggested" goes back to the straight line). Under From and To are Off blocks (UTC; leave it for now) and Altitude (− and + in 500 ft steps), and each end's weather against your limits (Go, Caution or No-go, for now until an hour after you land: tap one for the full check). Alternate is where you'd divert from your destination: GA-EFB suggests one (the nearest civil airfield, or a larger one with its services within 25 nm), with its track, distance, time and the extra fuel, which goes into the fuel required in the Plog and Pre-flight; tap it to choose another, None to leave it out, or Suggest one to go back. The Route check lists the NOTAMs for your flight too, with any airspace or hazard warnings on the route (Read them opens the briefing). The route check beside it shows the airspace and terrain along it, with a suggested altitude (in the USA with the FAA's airspace and its rules: a clearance for Class B, radio contact for C and D), and updates as you change the route: drag the + in the middle of a leg to add a point there, drag a point to move it (dropped on an airfield or reporting point, it becomes that one), tap an airfield for Add to route (it goes into the leg it's nearest) or Direct, tap a reporting point (a triangle) for Add to route, or search for a point to add. Reverse turns the route round, Suggest fills in a new route between the first and last airfields, Undo takes back the last change, and Import opens a plan from Little Navmap or SkyDemon. Send to sim sends the route to MSFS as a flight plan: with the GA-EFB Connector running it's saved on the sim PC (Documents\GA-EFB\Flight plans) and loaded into the sim if it's running (otherwise load it from the world map); without the Connector the .pln file is shared from your iPad to put on the PC yourself. The plog has the same button. Then Pre-flight for the go/no-go, or Plog for the plog sheet: headings, times and fuel with forecast winds. Once the route's made, the map clears to a bar along the top with your route, its distance, time and altitude, the route check's verdict (Clear, or how many things to watch: tap it for the full check) and Edit to change the route again (Done closes it). On an iPhone the bar is along the bottom.
  2. Weather. A card for your departure and destination (and any airfield you add), checked against your limits: GO, CAUTION or NO-GO, with what decides it and the hours ahead. Tap a card for the details.
  3. NOTAMs along your route, from the UK NOTAM feed.
  4. Weight & Balance. Pick the aircraft, or let GA-EFB read it from the sim. Enter people, baggage and fuel (the plog's fuel can come straight across) and check the weight and centre of gravity against the envelope. The take-off and landing tabs work out the distance you need for the runway, surface and weather.
  5. Daylight at your destination, so you're down before it's dark.
  6. Checklists for the aircraft you're flying, from the pre-flight inspection to shutdown, with the emergencies one tap away.
  7. Timer: along the top, UTC and local time, sunset (where the aircraft is, else at your destination) and your arrival time from the plog. Then three timers:
    • Flight: counting by itself from take-off, with your take-off time, the plog's next waypoint and landing times, and countdowns to the next FREDA check (every 15 minutes) and fuel check (every 30, with the fuel on board and endurance), the same as the reminders.
    • Stopwatch: tap Leg at each waypoint for that leg's time. With a plog loaded, each leg is named after it (EGCJ → Selby).
    • Countdown: 2, 5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes, or a minute more or less, with a ring that empties as it runs and the time it ends. Give it a name (＋ Name, e.g. Hold) and its notification says what it's for, whichever app you're in.
  8. Training: the UK PPL syllabus, laid out like Checklists: its six sections down the left, each with how many of its exercises you've done (a ✓ once you're confident in them all), and the chosen section's exercises beside them (1 to 19, the qualifying cross-country and the skills test). Each has a short In the sim hint on practising it in MSFS. Tap an exercise once you've practised it in the sim, and again when you're confident; Reset this section clears your own marks in it. On an iPhone the sections are a menu. Some exercises are marked practised by themselves. From your Logbook ("from your Logbook"): air experience (your first flight), taxiing, take-offs, circuits, circuit emergencies (glide approaches and go-arounds), navigation (a cross-country of 20 nm or more) and the qualifying cross-country (a day's legs of 150 nm or more, landing at two other airfields). From the sim, while you fly with the GA-EFB Connector ("from the sim", with what it saw): straight and level (2 minutes within 100 ft and 5°), climbing, descending and levelling off, turning, slow flight, steep turns, a practice forced landing (a glide at about 60 kt from 1,500 ft or more above the ground, then a go-around low down) and navigation at lower levels (5 minutes at 500–1,200 ft above the ground); with the latest Connector, stalling and spin avoidance (the stall warning, then a recovery), reduced visibility and radio navigation (3 minutes tracking a VOR course on NAV 1) too. Confident is yours to mark, but the app suggests it: once the sim has seen an exercise flown within the PPL skills test's limits on three different days (height within 150 ft, heading within 10°, speed within 15 kt, and each exercise's own, such as the climb at 67 kt within 5 kt, the glide at 60 kt within 5 kt, or a stall recovered within 10 seconds losing less than 300 ft), or your circuits were flown with nothing to work on, it shows Looks confident, and a tap marks it confident. Your circuits, landings, cross-countries and hours come from the Logbook too. It's kept with your account on all your devices. It's practice to go with your real lessons, not a training record.

In the air, GA-EFB follows you from the sim (with the Connector):

  • The Kneeboard opens by itself on take-off (beside the map if you're on the Map, otherwise the Kneeboard app, which is handy in Split View): your next waypoint, heading, times, fuel and endurance, FREDA and fuel checks when they're due, and who to talk to, all on one screen, with a scratchpad on its own tab.
  • The VFR Plog follows you leg by leg, with your actual times and fuel against the plan.
  • The Map is where the app opens. It has two modes, switched at the top: Plan (building the route, above) and Fly, which comes on by itself when you take off. In Fly it shows your aircraft, your route, airports and their layouts, with rain and cloud. Turn it with two fingers: while it's turned, a compass at the top of the right-hand buttons turns it back to north up; Track up in Map display (once the sim is connected) keeps your track pointing up as you fly. Along the top are your ground speed, track, altitude, the next waypoint and the time to it, your ETA, fuel and endurance, and the wind (the Map display button on the right, two sliders, opens switches for them, the dark map, track up and the glide range: turn the figures off for more map; on an iPhone, the small arrow above the right-hand buttons folds them away and back). With a plog route, the side-view button on the right (a hill under a line) opens the route from the side along the bottom, with the ground, the airspace you'll pass through or under and where you are; tap its bar to close it. GA-EFB warns you about controlled airspace and danger areas on your track a few minutes ahead (you can turn this off in Settings → Simulator). The button at the top of the map's toolbar shows the map in use (VFR, MAP or SAT): tap it for the Map Presets, VFR Chart, IFR (USA, Germany, France), Streets or Satellite. IFR is an en-route map of the USA (from the FAA's data), Germany (from DFS's, the German air navigation service) and France with its overseas territories (from the SIA's, France's aeronautical information service; filled triangles are compulsory reporting points, open ones on request). Much of France's and Germany's upper airspace is free route airspace, where there are no airways and aircraft fly direct between published points: on the High map those areas are outlined in dashed violet and labelled "FREE ROUTE (FRA)" with their levels, so an empty area there is expected. Zoomed out, the IFR map shows the airways and the waypoints on them; zoom in to about county level and the other waypoints the charts carry for direct routing appear too, with each airway segment's minimum altitude and length in nautical miles ("FL65 34": in the USA the FAA's minimum en-route altitude, in France and Germany the airway's lower limit). Controlled airspace, danger, restricted and prohibited areas show faintly behind the airways, as on an en-route chart; tap one to see what it is. Elsewhere, including the UK, the IFR map shows the airways openAIP has as faint blue corridors with their names (only as complete as openAIP's volunteers have made them, and without the waypoints, minimum altitudes or lengths). Flying an IFR plan: plan the flight in SimBrief as usual (it chooses the airways, SIDs and STARs anywhere in the world), then in the Map's Plan mode tap SimBrief on the route bar (or From SimBrief under the route) to load it. The map then draws it over the airways: each leg labelled with its airway, the SID and STAR dashed, and each waypoint named as you zoom in (tap one for its planned level). In flight the leg you're on turns pink, the legs flown grey, and the boxes along the top give the next waypoint, the distance and time to it, and your ETA at the destination. While a SimBrief plan is loaded it is your route on every map, on all your devices: Plan mode shows it (tap its bar to open it in the SimBrief app) instead of the route editor, an airfield's card offers only Direct to, and it becomes your route in the other apps too (Pre-flight, Weather, NOTAMs, the Kneeboard, Radio Calls and the rest), with its cruise level, speed and fuel; the route or circuits you had are kept and come back when you unload it. The bar also reminds you how the plan arrives, such as "Arrive via BNN1X, expect runway 27L", and the Kneeboard's Next tab shows the departure and arrival runways, SID and STAR SimBrief planned with (the runways in use come from the ATIS and ATC on the day). Tap Unload to plan your own route again; when you've made a newer plan in SimBrief, Load newer swaps to it. The map's credits name it ("Route: your SimBrief plan", with the navigation data cycle SimBrief used). Without a Navigraph subscription SimBrief plans with older navigation data, so the first time such a plan shows on the IFR map, a note says how many cycles old it is: a few of its waypoints may not line up with the current airways or the sim's own data: the airways (blue on the Low map, Victor and T routes; green on the High map, Jet and Q routes: the switch at the top) with their names, the waypoints (a black star) and reporting points (a triangle) with theirs, the control centres' boundaries (dashed brown), the navaids and the airports, on a pale map; zoom in to see the waypoints and their names. The cloud button opens the weather: off, rain, clouds, both, or Winds (winds aloft, shown on its own): the wind flows across the map as moving white streaks over a see-through colour wash of its strength (blue calm, through green and yellow, to red and purple strong; the key is bottom left, folded until you tap it, with the height and time shown beside it). Pick the height on the right: tap the height in use for the others (1,000 to 10,000 ft), and the list folds away again once you've chosen; the key and the heights hide while the timeline plays, and the time on the timeline along the bottom (now to two days ahead; ▶ plays through it, Now comes back). Tap anywhere on the map for the wind and temperature at every height there; zoomed well in, the figures show at points across the map too. The VFR chart covers the UK, most of Europe (from Ireland and Iceland to Portugal, Scandinavia, Greece and Cyprus, with the Canary Islands, Madeira, the Azores and the Faroe Islands; not Russia, Belarus or Ukraine), the USA, Canada, Latin America and the Caribbean, Africa, the Middle East, South Asia, Asia Pacific (Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Hong Kong, South-East Asia, Indonesia, the Philippines, Papua New Guinea and the Pacific islands, Central Asia and Mongolia; not mainland China), Australia and New Zealand: the land in a soft sage green with woods and forests in a darker green and the slopes shaded (more deeply as you zoom in) so the hills and mountains stand out; towns in yellow, lakes and rivers in blue, and roads in chart colours (motorways and A-roads orange, B-roads yellow, minor roads white) with railways and town names; the airspace below FL100 (controlled airspace, ATZs, MATZs, danger areas, drop zones and gliding sites) with its name, class and top over base, as on a paper chart; the reporting points (a filled triangle is compulsory); the radio navaids (VORs with their compass rose turned to magnetic north, NDBs and DMEs, each with a box giving its name, frequency and ident); and the airfields, a ring with the main runway across it where it's hard (blue for military), with the name and elevation (no frequency on the chart: an online controller may use a different one). Zoomed in, each airfield's grounds show in grey, and closer still its layout in the chart's own colours (darker on the night chart): runways with their numbers, taxiways with their letters, aprons, and terminals in blue. Tap an area for everything there, or an airfield for its card. It covers the UK, and in the USA the map and its airspace from the FAA (Class B solid blue, Class C solid magenta, Class D dashed blue, Class E surface areas dashed magenta, and restricted areas, MOAs and other special use airspace); its airfields (public ones; private strips are left out) and navaids too; outside the UK and the USA, the airspace, reporting points and navaids come from openAIP and the airfields from OurAirports, anywhere in the world (beyond those areas, such as mainland China, Russia and the open ocean, over a plainer map, and only as complete as openAIP's data for that country); the USA has no reporting points (US charts mostly use landmarks instead). Zoom in for the airspace (labels and names from about county size). Zoomed right out (a continent or the world) the map shows the countries' names only; the airport codes appear once a country fills the screen, the larger airports first. Where small airfields crowd the map (Papua New Guinea's hundreds of bush strips, for example), they appear as you zoom in further, with the airports shown first. Labels never overlap: one moves to the other side of its symbol if it can, or else is left out (the symbol stays), so zoom in further to see them all. With the chart on, the layers button opens a column of layer buttons beside it, blue when on: airspace, airfields, reporting points, navaids, terrain, and roads, railways and towns (off leaves just the land, water and aviation). The settings cog has switches for the weather at airfields, the NOTAMs on your route and SimBrief's route (shown while a SimBrief plan is loaded), the airport codes and the airport layouts (the arrow on the toolbar follows your aircraft). Your choices are remembered on all your devices. The map's credits sit in a banner along the bottom with "For flight simulation only" and the map's own credit (tap it for all the credits; on an iPhone it's hidden while a panel such as NRST covers the bottom of the map). The Dark map switch (in Map display) makes the map dark: the VFR chart in its night colours (near-black land, dim towns and woods, bright roads), the IFR map dark too, or, with the chart off, the Dark map instead of Streets. The app's own light or dark look is in Settings, and Night Red is in the Control Centre. Pinch to zoom (while following your aircraft, the map zooms in and out about it). The compass button under the settings cog resets the map: north up, back on your aircraft at the usual zoom (and following it), or else on your route or where you last were. The arrow under it follows your aircraft (filled while it does; moving the map yourself stops it). When the sim isn't connected, it shows your route instead (or where your aircraft last was, else your home airfield), with a note saying whether the GA-EFB Connector or the sim isn't running and a link to setting it up. In the air a ring shows how far the Cessna 152 could glide from your height (about 1.5 nm for every 1,000 ft above the ground, carried downwind by the wind), with the nearest airfield and whether it's within reach; its switch in Map display hides it. The Kneeboard button (it opens by itself at take-off) shows beside the map, in three tabs: Next, your next radio call (the one for where you are in the flight, filled in like Radio Calls), the next waypoint with its track, heading, time and ETA, when the next FREDA and fuel checks are due, and your radio frequency; Nav log, the route with the actual time and fuel at each waypoint and the ETAs from your ground speed; and Checks, the checks due, FREDA, and the way to your checklists and scratchpad. With your aircraft in the air, ground within 1,000 ft below you is shaded amber, and ground within 100 ft of you or above you red (Terrain warning in Map display turns it off). Airfields that report the weather have a coloured ring from their METAR: green VFR, blue marginal VFR, red IFR, magenta low IFR (Weather at airfields under the settings cog). GA-EFB also says its alerts aloud: airspace ahead or entered, the fuel and FREDA checks, sunset, the aircraft's warnings, "Approaching Harewood" a mile from each waypoint and the next leg's heading as you pass it (Settings → Notifications → Spoken Alerts turns it off). The NRST button on the right (a runway) lists the nearest airfields (from your aircraft, or the middle of the map without the sim) with their magnetic bearing, distance, longest runway and whether they're within gliding reach, each with Direct to; tap one's name for its card. The magnifying glass at the top of the right-hand buttons finds an airport or airfield by its code, name or town: pick one and the map flies there and opens its card. Tap an airfield for its card: its weather against your limits, the runway into wind and the crosswind on it (tap another runway in its list to see the wind on that one instead), the TAF as a timeline, its runways and frequencies, and Arriving: each runway's circuit direction, the usual circuit height on QNH, PPR, private, parachuting, opening hours and remarks, where openAIP has them (always check the airfield's own published details). Direct to makes it your route from where you are, as a GPS's Direct To: in flight, from your aircraft's present position (when the airfield, or a waypoint you tap on your route, is already on the route, straight to it and on with the rest); on the ground, from your departure (else your home airfield). The figures along the top and the nav log follow it. Set as destination changes where you're going (diverting), Set as departure (on the ground) where you start from, and Add to route puts it into the leg it's nearest. A reporting point you tap offers Add to route too, and Direct to in flight. Each change has Undo on its note. To plan a route, switch to Plan. The route's NOTAMs show on the map too (red for hazards such as danger areas and parachuting, orange for obstacles, grey for the rest): tap one to read it. Grey notices covering more than 10 nm (a radio beacon out of service, GPS interference) aren't drawn, as a circle that size says nothing about where; they're in the briefing's list.
  • Radio Calls gives you the calls for each part of the flight, in the wording of where you're flying, filled in with your callsign, position, altitude and QNH (or altimeter, in the USA).
  • Diverting: the Map's NRST button (a runway, on the right) lists the nearest airfields, with Direct to on each.

While the app is open with the sim connected, it records your track. Open a flight in the Log to play it back: the track on the map (and the planned circuit, for circuits), the aircraft moving along it at 4 to 32 times the speed, and your height over time to scrub through.

While you fly, the Logbook shows the flight at the top (Track on map, and Details for its fuel, stats and landing report). After landing, GA-EFB writes the flight into your Logbook by itself once you shut the engine down at the stand or close the sim (no need to press Complete Flight), with times, landings and night, and a report on each touchdown: rate, G, centreline and distance down the runway.

6. Circuit training

For circuits instead of a cross-country, choose Circuits at the top of the Map's Plan panel (or of the VFR Plog): your cross-country route is put aside and comes back when you choose Cross-country again. Your cross-country route is kept, and comes back when you switch back.

  1. Pick the airfield: type its code or name (any airfield), or tap your home airfield.
  2. The runway is the one most into wind, using the sim's own wind when you're there with it running, else the METAR. Each runway shows its headwind and crosswind, so you can pick another. A runway missing (small airfields' grass strips sometimes are)? Tap A runway missing? and add it: its number, length and surface.
  3. Check the circuit direction and height. The default is left-hand at 1,000 ft above the airfield. Where openAIP records the direction it's used instead. The real circuit for each runway is in the airfield's AIP entry or on its website: if it's different, change it once, and GA-EFB remembers it for that runway on all your devices. It also works out the other end of the runway, since the circuit usually stays on the same side (set 28 left-hand and 10 becomes right-hand), and a grass strip beside it.
  4. Plan the circuits: how many, and what each one is — normal, flapless, glide approach or go-around. The last one ends with a full-stop landing.

GA-EFB works out each leg's heading for the wind, height, speed and time, plus the fuel for the session. On the plog's Circuit tab, This circuit sums it up beside the airfield and runway: the wind on the runway and the cloud base, the five legs' headings and heights, which side of the runway the circuit and the deadside are, and how to join. The circuit itself, with the deadside, the overhead join and the ATZ, is drawn on the Map (in Plan and Fly, as a route is) and the Kneeboard. Pre-flight changes to suit: the weather is checked against your limits for the crosswind on your runway and the cloud base against circuit height, and the airspace and NOTAMs are checked round the circuit.

In the air, the Kneeboard shows which leg you're on, the circuit number, the heading, height and speed to fly, what to do next, and the radio call. Radio Calls has a Circuits tab too. After each circuit you get a short report: the height of the crosswind turn, your height and spacing on downwind, the height of the final turn, your speed on final, and the touchdown (rate, where on the runway, and the centreline). On an iPad the reports sit side by side in one table on the plog's Circuit tab (the latest four, with Earlier circuits for the rest). On an iPad every Circuits tab (Circuit, Legs, Session & fuel, Airspace) fits the screen without scrolling: the ⓘ notes open over the page, and if there's more airspace than fits, a button shows the rest; on an iPhone each circuit is one line, and a tap shows its report. Touch-and-goes, go-arounds and the full stop are counted by themselves, and the session goes in your logbook.

7. The apps

The first time you sign in, after the "For flight simulation only" notice, a short welcome sets up your aircraft, your home airport and the GA-EFB Connector (skip any step and set it later in W&B and Settings; it isn't shown again, on any of your devices). The app opens on the Map the first time, and after that wherever you left it; swipe up from the bar at the bottom of any app to go to the home screen. The light-aircraft apps are on the home screen from the start. The extras marked Extra below (the airliner and add-on tools) aren't: get any of them from the Apps app and it joins the home screen. To remove one, tap Edit at the top of Apps, then Remove beside it, and Done (Settings and Apps stay).

Pre-flight
Everything before a flight on one screen, as a briefing pack: one answer at the top (GO, CAUTION or NO-GO, and what to watch), then a card each for the weather at both ends against your limits, the route, airspace and terrain, NOTAMs, weight and balance with the envelope, fuel (taxi, trip, reserve, needed and on board), performance, daylight and checklists. Mark each card checked as you go.
VFR Plog
The plog sheet for the route you planned on the Map: headings, times and fuel with forecast winds, followed leg by leg in the air, with the aircraft settings (altitude, cruise, departure time) and Edit on Map, Pre-flight and Send to sim at the top (Unload, top right, clears the route). With no route yet, Plan on the Map takes you there; Route files opens a SkyDemon or Little Navmap plan. Or circuits at any airfield, planned a step at a time: the airfield (the airfields you've used lately are under its search), the runway (the one most into wind marked; tapping one moves straight on), the direction and height, how many circuits, then a check with the circuit drawn; + New starts again (Unload, beside it, clears the circuits), with tabs for the circuit, its legs, the session and fuel, and the airspace round it. To keep routes you fly again, turn on Saved Plans in Settings → Planning.
Kneeboard
In the air at a glance, on one screen: next waypoint, heading, ETAs, fuel and endurance, checks due and frequencies. Your scratchpad, a blank page to write on, is on its own tab.
Radio Calls
Radio calls for each part of a flight, filled in with your callsign, position, altitude and pressure setting, in the wording of where you're flying: the UK (Basic Service, MATZ, overhead joins), Europe and the rest of the world (the international ICAO wording: flight information, control zone crossings, VFR entry points), the USA (flight following, Class Bravo, self-announcing on the CTAF, altimeter in inches) or Australia (CTAF broadcasts, "received" the ATIS, flight following from Centre). Picked from your departure airfield; change it in the details at the top. VFR or IFR at the top of the calls: IFR has the clearance and its readback, the first call to Departure, checking in, level changes, direct routings, weather deviations and position reports, the first call to Approach, holding, established on the approach, the missed approach and cancelling IFR. It's each region's standard wording, not every airfield's local procedures.
WBSim C152
The WBSim Cessna 152's own jobs: chocks, engine and pitot covers, engine condition and maintenance.
W&B
Weight and balance with the centre-of-gravity envelope, and take-off and landing distances. Three aircraft come built in: the Cessna 152 and the Cessna 172S Skyhawk (each with its loading figures and its take-off and landing tables) and the Piper PA-28-161 Warrior (its loading figures; type in the take-off and landing distances from its handbook's graphs). Any other aircraft can be added with New aircraft. The one you choose is picked by itself when the sim's aircraft matches it, and the Plog's cruise speed and fuel burn follow a 152 or 172S you choose here.
Weather
A card for each airfield, readable at a glance: your plog's departure and destination (or where you are, else your home airfield), then any you add with the search at the top (an ICAO code, a name or a town; the ✕ removes one). Each card shows GO, CAUTION or NO-GO against your limits, the one thing that decides it, the cloud base, visibility, wind with the crosswind on the best runway, and QNH, a bar for the next few hours (green, amber or red, with "Improving", "Worsening" or "Settled") and small notes such as fog risk, carb icing or sunset. Tap a card for everything else: each finding in plain words, the hours ahead from the TAF, the METAR and TAF as written, the winds aloft, its NOTAMs, and your limits. Limits beside "Your flight" changes them too. Rain radar and clouds are on the Map.
Checklists
Normal and emergency checklists for the Cessna 152, Cessna 172S and Piper PA-28, and your own. Pick a checklist on the left, then tap each item, or press Mark item as complete to tick the next one; when it's done, the same button takes you to the next checklist.
Map
Plan your route and fly it. Plan: From and To, the route round the airspace filled in, the route check beside it. Fly (by itself at take-off): the moving map that turns with two fingers, with your route, airports, airport layouts, airspace, rain and cloud, your flight's figures along the top, the Kneeboard beside it, NRST for the nearest airfields, and the route from the side along the bottom. Direct to, Set as destination and Add to route on an airfield's card change your route.
Charts
Your own aerodrome and VFR charts, ready to draw on. Your last five airfield searches sit under the search box (Recent), the same on all your devices: tap one to open it again. To add one, type the airfield's code and tap Add a chart: choose what kind it is (Aerodrome, VFR, Departure, Arrival, Approach or Other), the runway for a departure, arrival or approach, then a screenshot, photo or PDF (up to six pages), and check the name it suggests (such as "Approach RWY 24"). Your charts go in the same tabs as the FAA's (an Aerodrome chart under GND, Departure under SID, Arrival under STAR, Approach under APP, Other under GEN), marked Yours where there are FAA charts too, and split by runway, and open in the same viewer: pinch to zoom, rotate, draw on it, night mode; the ⋯ button renames or deletes one. They're on all your devices: the one you add a chart on keeps the original (a PDF as it is, a picture at full size), sharp however far you zoom in, and your other devices show a smaller copy. For US airports it also lists the FAA's charts, free and updated every 28 days: the airport diagram, departures, arrivals and approach plates. Type the airport's code (KJFK, or the FAA's own, such as 02T): its charts are in tabs, each over a coloured bar: GEN (general), GND (the airport), VFR (when there are VFR charts), SID (departures), STAR (arrivals) and APP (approaches); a tab with no charts is dimmed, opening on Departure for your plog's departure and Approach for its destination. Departures and approaches are grouped by runway, and each approach has a coloured badge for its kind (ILS, RNAV, VOR...). Typing in the search box looks through all the tabs. Tap a chart to open it: on an iPad it opens beside the list, which stays there for the next one; on an iPhone it fills the screen, edge to edge, with the list in a panel along the bottom: drag the panel's bar up or down to any height (it settles down, half way or nearly full), or tap Charts / Hide, or a tab.
Logbook
The flight you're flying at the top, logged by itself when it's over; every flight with times, landings and night, totals and 90-day currency, and the plog as flown. Each flight has a Replay (its track on the map, with the height underneath) and a Debrief: a mark out of 100, what went well and what to work on, from how steadily you held your height and heading in level flight (against the PPL skills test's ±150 ft and ±10°), your steepest descent, the landing (how firm, how near the centreline, how far along the runway, any bounce), your leg times and fuel against the plog, and your circuits. It needs the app open with the sim connected during the flight, and is a guide for practice in the sim, not an examiner's verdict. The Landings tab has every landing saved: rate, G, wind and where you touched down, by month (the latest open; tap a month to open or fold it, as with flights).
Documents
Your PDFs, such as your aircraft's POH, always to hand.
Notes
Your notes, typed or drawn: clearances, squawks, ATIS, anything. The drawing page is the Kneeboard's scratchpad too, so what you jot down in one is in the other.
SimBrief Extra
Your latest SimBrief flight plan: route, weather, NOTAMs, fuel, weights and times. The flight's rules (IFR or VFR, from the plan) show beside the callsign at the top; the badge on each weather card (VFR, MVFR, IFR or LIFR conditions) is the airfield's weather from its METAR, not the flight's rules.
GSX Control, Add-ons Extra
GSX Pro's menu (jetway, catering, fuel, boarding, pushback), and your sim add-ons.
Conversion Extra
Fuel, distance, speed, pressure and temperature conversions.
Settings, Apps
Your details, preferences and connections; adding and removing apps.

8. Getting around the EFB

GA-EFB works like an iPad:

  • Home screen: app icons, the dock along the bottom, and widgets for today's flight, the weather and pre-flight. Press and hold to move icons, make folders or change widgets. To get back to it from any app, swipe up from just above the bottom of the screen (a swipe from the very bottom edge leaves GA-EFB, as it does any app).
  • Search: swipe down on the home screen to find apps, settings, airports, your saved plans and documents.
  • Quick Controls: tap the plane and Connector icons at the top right for brightness, dark or light, Night Red, the background and lock (with a passcode), whether the sim and GA-EFB are connected, and your notifications. Swipe it up, or tap outside it, to put it away. The plane in GA-EFB's top bar shows the sim at a glance: green when it's connected, red and crossed out when it isn't.
  • Tabs: the bigger apps (Weather, the Kneeboard, Apps) are split into tabs along the top, so each part fits on the screen without scrolling. Each app remembers the tab you were on. On a landscape iPad, the Logbook and Settings show their list on the left and what you've chosen on the right, and Pre-flight lays its steps out in two columns; a finished step folds to one line.
  • Notifications (Settings → Alerts) remind you of your departure time and sunset, warn you about airspace ahead and bring you each landing report. Red badges on app icons show what needs a look.

9. Your account and your devices

  • The same everywhere. Your logbook, notes, plogs, checklists and settings go with your GA-EFB account, so sign in on your iPad and iPhone and they show the same things.
  • Backups. Settings → Back Up & Restore saves everything (including your photos, wallpaper and charts) to one file, and puts it back.
  • Forgotten your password? Use Forgotten your password? on the sign-in screen: we email you a link, which opens a page where you choose a new one; then sign in with it. Settings → your name lets you change it (Change Password emails you the same link), sign out of all your devices, or delete your account. Made your account with Apple? There's no password to forget: press Continue with Apple again, and deleting the account asks Apple to confirm it's you.

10. Updates

The app updates through the App Store, like any other app. New versions of the GA-EFB Connector are on its download page: install it over the top of the old one.

11. Your trial and subscription

  • Your 7-day free trial starts the first time you use the app. Settings → Subscription shows when it ends.
  • Subscribe in the app, from Settings → Subscription or when the trial ends: £24.99 a year or £3.99 a month, through the App Store. It unlocks on all your devices signed in to your GA-EFB account.
  • Cancel or change plan any time from Settings → Subscription → Manage Subscription, or your Apple ID's subscriptions. It carries on until the end of the period you've paid for.
  • New iPad, or reinstalled? Sign in with the same account; Restore Purchases in Settings → Subscription brings back a subscription bought with this Apple ID.
  • Everything from your trial is kept. Refunds for subscriptions are Apple's: see the refund policy.

12. Problems and how to fix them

The app can't find the Connector
  • Check the iPad or iPhone and the PC are on the same Wi-Fi (not a guest network or mobile data).
  • Check the Connector is running: open GA-EFB Connector from the Start menu. Its window shows whether it can see MSFS and, if Windows treats your network as Public (which blocks the iPad), says so with a button to change it.
  • If it says "The Internet connection appears to be offline" or "Couldn't reach the GA-EFB Connector", GA-EFB may not be allowed on your local network: on the iPad or iPhone open Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network and turn GA-EFB on, then scan a new code.
  • The Windows firewall may be blocking it. Open Windows Security → Firewall & network protection → Allow an app through firewall, find Node.js, and tick Private. Also check Windows treats your home Wi-Fi as a Private network (Settings → Network & internet → your Wi-Fi).
  • Still not connecting? Unpair it in the Connector and in Settings → Simulator, then pair again.
It says the simulator is offline

The Connector connects to MSFS 2024 by itself once the sim is running. Give it a few seconds, and check you're in a flight rather than the main menu. The Connector's page shows whether it can see MSFS.

I subscribed but it hasn't unlocked

It usually takes a few seconds, and needs the internet. Go to Settings → Subscription (or the screen asking you to subscribe) and press Restore Purchases. If the subscription was bought while signed in to a different GA-EFB account, sign in with that one. Still stuck? Email us.

No clouds on the map

Clouds come from the weather satellites (Meteosat over Europe and Africa, GOES over the Americas, Himawari over Asia and Australia) and need no key; outside Europe they're about an hour old, and nothing shows near the poles: turn them on with the map's weather button (Clouds, or Both for rain on top). They can take a minute to appear, and need an internet connection. If the satellite service is down, a free OpenWeatherMap key (Settings → Weather & Limits) gives a simpler cloud layer instead.
